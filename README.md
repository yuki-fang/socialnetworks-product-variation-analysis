# Social Networks Analysis: Analyzing How Product Variations Influence Purchase Intent, Customer Clusters, & Cannibalization Risk

## Project Motivation
Jellycat’s Bartholomew Bear is a flagship product with multiple variations (e.g., Bathrobe Bear, Birthday Cake Bear). While product extensions can attract new customers, they also carry the risk of **cannibalizing the original**.  
This project explores whether Jellycat’s Bartholomew Bear variations **expand the customer base** or **shift demand away** from the original bear.

## Data
- **Source**: Social media co-engagement data on Jellycat product pages and variations.  
- **Observations**: Customer overlaps in engagement between the original Bartholomew Bear and its product variations.  
- **Format**: Audience duplication matrices, later transformed into a network dataset for visualization and clustering.  

##  Methodology
1. **Clustering Analysis** – Grouped customer segments based on engagement overlap across product variations.  
2. **Audience Duplication Analysis** – Measured the degree of overlap between the original bear and its variations.  
3. **Network Visualization (Gephi)** – Mapped co-engagement to identify communities and at-risk clusters.  

## Key Findings
- **Uniqueness-focused buyers**: Favor distinct variations (e.g., themed bears) while still purchasing the original → **complementary demand**.  
- **Collectability-focused buyers**: Show lower intent for the original and higher risk of shifting to certain variations (e.g., Bathrobe Bear) → **cannibalization risk**.  

These insights help Jellycat understand which variations **strengthen the brand** and which require careful management to avoid **eroding the original’s market share**.

## Tools
- **Gephi** → Network analysis & visualization  
- **Excel** → Data preparation & matrix construction
